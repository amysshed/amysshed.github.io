La Boca

05/04/2026

It’s been almost two months since I spent half a day in La Boca, and I’m still thinking about it. My friends and I went on a free walking tour of the area in Buenos Aires, and I fell in love. The colourful buildings painted with left-over ship paint, the art on every corner, and the strong sense of community made my creative soul swoon.

We learnt about Quinquela, the man who gained the title “The painter of La Boca”—an inspiring figure who captured his community in grand oil paintings and, after gaining success, ensured that his community would have access to the art world by asking artists to create permanent pieces of art on the streets of La Boca for all to see.

Standing in Quinquela’s old flat on the top floor of the museum, I thought of the paintings my dad loves — depicting Glasgow shipyards. Quinquela captures similar subjects but uses vibrant colour and thick palette knife strokes to create sun-soaked energy.

Maybe it highlights the difference between the cities — one filled with economic promise and another plagued with abandonment.

Within the museum, there was a collection of amazing pieces by Argentinian artists. My favourite was Plena tarea by Mario Baima (1975).
